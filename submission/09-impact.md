For any "impact" or "who benefits" box.

**Who it helps.** A parent deciding whether the child walks to school. A school deciding whether assembly is inside or on the ground. A housing society deciding whether to hold an outdoor event.

**What changes for them.** They get one answer for one named place, with the monitor, its distance and the reading's age, instead of one city number. They see when they should not trust it: a reading over six hours old gives no advice, and an estimate says it is an estimate.

**Evidence it works, from real days.**

- 21 Oct 2025, the day after Diwali: Home read AQI 500 from a monitor 3.8 km away, and the app said avoid going out.
- 9 Nov 2025: Home 424 (Stage III likely) and the park 249 (Stage II likely) were four kilometres apart. One city number would have hidden that.
- 31 Oct 2025: the alert counted 85 satellite fires in the last 24 hours, near Home or upwind, and said smoke could reach it within a day or two.
- These are replayed from public archives (OpenAQ, Open-Meteo, NASA FIRMS) in the app itself, labelled "recorded". The stage is my estimate, never the official order.

**Why others can use it.** It is free, with no sign-in. It costs about $4.50 a month idle at list prices and $0.0008 an assistant question, so a school or society can run its own copy. The code is public (https://github.com/hrsh-kr/the-vayu-project) with 1,257 tests, and the app is live at https://d3lmq1mze2gup3.cloudfront.net.

**Honest limits.** I have not run a pilot, so I make no claim of users or health outcomes. The unit has not been compared with a monitor. The cleaner I built (about 191 cfm of airflow, ₹5,000 of parts) has no CADR measured.

**What would show impact next.** The unit beside a monitor for a few days, with the difference published. The smoke-decay test for the cleaner. One school using it for a term.
