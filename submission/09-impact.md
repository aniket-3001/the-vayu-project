**Who benefits.** A parent deciding whether a child walks to school. A school deciding whether assembly is inside or on the ground. A housing society deciding whether to hold an event outdoors. Each gets one answer for one named place, with the monitor, its distance and the reading's age, instead of one number for a whole city. They also see when not to trust it: a reading over six hours old gives no advice, and an estimate is labelled an estimate.

**What the recorded days show** (real archive data, played as if it were 8 am):
- **21 Oct 2025, the day after Diwali:** Home read AQI 500 (PM2.5 980 µg/m³) from a monitor 3.8 km away. The official GRAP stage for all of NCR was II; the band for that place's own air was IV, by my estimate. The app said stay in and named 4 to 5 pm as the cleanest daylight hour.
- **9 Nov 2025:** two places 4 km apart averaged 434 and 319 on India's scale over 24 hours, the Stage III and Stage II bands. A city number hides that gap.
- **31 Oct 2025:** the app counted 85 satellite fires in the previous 24 hours and warned that smoke could arrive.

**Why it can spread.** It is free, needs no install or account, and costs about $4.50 a month idle, so a school or society could run its own copy.

**What is not proven.** There is no pilot and there are no users yet, so I claim no health outcome. Next: one school for one term.
