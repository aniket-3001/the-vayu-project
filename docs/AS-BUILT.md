# As built

What the app is today, what is built but switched off, what is not built, and where it is known to be weak. This is a current-state document: it says what is true now and has no history. The history is in `learning-log.md`. What was checked by hand on the live address, and what was not, is in `LIVE-CHECKLIST.md`.

Everything the live app and the recorded days show is real data. Made-up numbers exist only in test fixtures, and `make prove` fails if one reaches the deployed bundle.

## The screens

- **My Air.** Your places as cards in the colour of their air: a label and address, the AQI, the weather, the cleanest hour, today and the next two days, and the source with its distance and age. A card with a likely GRAP stage wears a pill. Above the cards, alerts (GRAP stage, smog, stubble burning, rain, wind) worst first, and tips for the day. With no places yet, a button opens the recorded days in a new tab.
- **Add a place.** Search (north India), or Detect my location, which asks for a name or offers to follow you (live location). A place called Home, School, Work or Park asks who it is for. Cards can be dragged into order. Places are saved on the device, or to the account when signed in (up to eight).
- **A place page.** The air and, beside it, Insights for this place: the alerts that apply, a school's morning assembly, the best time for a walk in a park, the cleanest daylight hour. Then the hourly forecast, the air pollutants and the next five days.
- **Exposure.** The day as three labelled lines (home, school or work, live outdoor location), each hour a block coloured by AQI, and Your day in the air. People in a family each have a day. Outdoor time can be logged by hand or by telling the assistant. A measured hour from the unit replaces the outside reading.
- **Map.** Every monitor the app can read, wind, fires and the air colour. A flame opens a popup, and Report this fire opens a sheet with the address, the coordinates, Copy location, Open in maps, and the numbers 112 and 101. The app contacts no one.
- **AIR Lens.** The air drawn as particles, with the camera behind when allowed and an illustration when not; a 36-hour window to slide or play.
- **Assistant.** Ask, or tell it something ("I walked in the park for 40 minutes"), in English or Hindi.
- **Your room.** With the measuring unit added (Add device, then Bluetooth), the first card shows the room's live reading against the air outside.
- **Account.** A button on My Air opens the Account page: who is signed in, when the last copy was saved, Sign out (which removes the places from this device; the copy stays in the account), and Delete my account (the copy and the account, after a second confirmation).
- **Switches.** GPS (the app's own switch for using the device's location) and a relatable measure (cigarettes a day, from Berkeley Earth's rule of thumb of 22 µg/m³ for a day, shown as a rule of thumb).

Times read in am and pm throughout. The colour scale says it is the US AQI scale.

## The recorded days (`/#/week`)

Seven real days, each played as "now" at 8 am India time: 7 Oct, 21 Oct, 31 Oct, 9 Nov and 22 Dec 2025, and 23 Jan and 30 Mar 2026. It starts with two places, Home (a spot near R K Puram) and Lodhi Garden, and one person.

- **Real:** PM2.5 and PM10 from four Delhi monitors (OpenAQ public archive), weather (Open-Meteo archive), gases (CAMS through Open-Meteo), fires (NASA FIRMS VIIRS). `scripts/record-week.mjs` writes `apps/web/src/week/recorded.json` (about 240 KB, its own chunk).
- **Hindsight, not forecast.** The hours after the moment are what was recorded next, so rain, wind and best-hour advice on a recorded day look better than a forecast made at the time would. The page says so.
- **Only four monitors.** The nearest monitor for a place is the nearest of those four, so a distance such as "Lodhi Road, 3.8 km" belongs to the recording. The live app chooses from about 7,000 monitors and would often find a closer one.
- **Cleaning is two rules.** A PM2.5 of 999 or more, a PM10 of 2,000 or more, or a PM10 below the same hour's PM2.5 is dropped. A monitor's hour that reads over three times the median of at least two others, and is itself high (PM2.5 over 200, PM10 over 500), is dropped as a lone spike: 86 hours across the seven days, counted in the file. That second rule could remove a genuine local spike, such as one next to a firework display, so the count is published.
- **No place or person of yours is touched.** Places, people and anything added live in memory; the assistant and the voice are the real AWS ones, answering about the recorded day. Leaving reloads the page.

## Known limits

- **Two scales.** The AQI on a card is the US EPA formula applied to PM2.5, and the colour scale says so. A GRAP stage is worked out on India's CPCB scale from the last 24 hours (the alert says "on India's scale"). They are different numbers: US AQI 200 is about 150 µg/m³ of PM2.5, and CPCB 200 is about 90. A card can read Moderate on the US scale while the CPCB 24-hour average is already in a GRAP band, and the reverse. The US AQI also stops at 500, so the app shows it as 500+.
- **GRAP is per monitor, and can sit above the official stage.** On 21 Oct 2025 the official stage was II for all of NCR (Delhi's 24-hour average was 351), while one monitor at Lodhi Road read far higher, so the band for Home's air was IV. The alert now says "the air here is in the GRAP Stage IV band", not that the stage is likely, and points to the order. This was an overstatement in the first version of the alert wording.
- **GRAP is per monitor.** The official stage is set once for the whole of Delhi-NCR. The app works it out from the monitor nearest each place, so two places a few kilometres apart can show different stages on the same morning (the recorded 9 Nov does: Home Stage III, the park Stage II). The alert says so. A city-wide estimate would be more faithful and is not built.
- **GRAP is our estimate.** It is checked against the monitors' own PM10 on 24 station-days (22 in the same stage as the monitor's own index, 2 with no estimate because the monitor had too few recent hours), which tests the method's arithmetic. It is not checked against the CAQM orders, which are the real ground truth. Official orders come first.
- **Advice thresholds are ours.** The stay-out lengths at AQI 100, 150 and 200, and the mask advice, are our words from the US AQI bands, not a medical rule or a CPCB or WHO table. The screen says so.
- **Exposure is an estimate.** It uses the timetable, an assumed indoor share of 60% of outdoor PM2.5 when there is no monitor in the room, and an assumed 85% in a park with trees. All are labelled "assumed".
- **The assistant's number check** catches a figure that is not in the facts, and a masked name that comes back. It also refuses an answer that calls the air good or clean when the level is not Good, one that spells a number out ("twice", "half", "fifty") or adds a word of reassurance ("safe", "no need to worry") that the rules did not write. It does not check where a number sits in the sentence, so a true number in the wrong place would pass. It cannot catch every wrong word, which is why the rules decide the advice and the model only rewords it.
- **Hindi is a different path.** Hindi answers never call the model: they are wording a person wrote with the facts slotted in. Questions are matched by keyword, in Devanagari, in Hindi typed in Latin letters, or in English, so unusual phrasing can fall back to a short status line with the AQI and the topics to ask about.
- **Who a caller is.** The per-caller limit hashes the address CloudFront reports in `CloudFront-Viewer-Address`. API Gateway's own address is public, so someone who calls it directly can write that header and dodge the per-caller limit. The whole-day limits and the API's throttle still hold, and they are what bound the cost. Closing it means checking a secret that only CloudFront knows; that is not done.
- **Caps are shared.** The 500 assistant questions and 200 request forms a day are for everyone. A script could use them up in minutes; the app then answers from the rules and says so. There is no per-address limit at the edge.
- **Tests against the real services.** The unit tests use fakes. What reaches the real services is `make smoke` against the live address, which asks the assistant a question and checks the answer carries the mask rule. It does not require the model's wording: a rules-only answer passes, and so does the assistant being switched off or at its limit, as designed. That the model rewords an answer was seen in the scripted browser run in `tests/live-check/` (the answer says "Wording by AI, checked against the facts"), which is not part of CI. Nothing in CI calls Bedrock.
- **Accounts are basic on purpose.** Last write wins: if two devices change the same things, the later save is kept. Signing in on a device that holds places replaces them with the account's (the screen says so). No two-factor, no sign-in with Google, no recovery other than the emailed code. The email comes from Cognito's own sender, which allows about 50 a day, so a burst of sign-ups can run into that. The password is sent to Cognito over HTTPS (`USER_PASSWORD_AUTH`) and is never seen by our API. The sign-in itself was not tried end to end by the build (no account is made on the live pool by a script); the pieces are covered by tests and by the live checks of the refusals.
- **The recorded days are shared and cannot be changed.** Everyone who opens them sees the same recording. A visitor can add places and people on top; those stay in that tab and are gone on reload. The starting places, the person and the days cannot be removed, nothing typed there is sent to the API, and a test freezes the data and plays every day to show nothing writes to it.
- **Browser support.** Bluetooth needs Chrome on a computer or Android, over HTTPS; not iPhone Safari. Tested on Chrome on a Mac only.

The forecast hours come from the CAMS model, which includes weather in its prediction, corrected with the nearest monitor's recent readings. The app does not adjust a measured number for weather.

## What the browser asks of other sites

The wind and the air-model colour are asked of the weather models once for everyone by our API, so a visitor's browser never spends Open-Meteo's per-address allowance. The browser still asks Open-Meteo, or Photon, to search for a place by name (it sends only the name typed), the same services to name an address, and OpenFreeMap for map tiles. In the recorded days the page asks for no air, wind or fires: they come from the recording.
